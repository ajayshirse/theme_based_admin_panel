How to Brush Properly: A Two-Minute Routine

Small changes to angle, pressure and timing make a big difference to plaque removal.

Most people brush for less than a minute. Aim for two full minutes, twice a day, with a soft-bristled brush and fluoride toothpaste.

Angle the bristles towards the gumline at about 45 degrees and use short, gentle strokes. Pressing hard does not clean better and can wear gums. Spend about thirty seconds on each quarter of your mouth, including the tongue-side of your teeth.

Replace your brush or brush head every three months, or sooner if the bristles splay.

Flossing Made Simple: Why and How

Cleaning between teeth removes plaque that a brush cannot reach.

A toothbrush cleans only about sixty percent of tooth surfaces. The rest are between teeth, where plaque builds up and can lead to cavities and gum disease.

Use a piece of floss about the length of your forearm. Curve it around each tooth in a C shape and slide it gently below the gumline. If floss is awkward, interdental brushes or water flossers are good alternatives.

Mild bleeding in the first week is common and usually settles as your gums become healthier.

Sugar, Snacks and Your Teeth

It is how often you have sugar, not just how much, that affects your teeth.

Each time you eat or drink something sugary, bacteria in plaque produce acid that softens enamel for up to an hour. Frequent snacking keeps your teeth under attack for longer.

Try to keep sweet foods to mealtimes, choose water between meals and finish with cheese, nuts or fruit rather than a sweet. Fruit juice and sports drinks count as sugary drinks.

Wait around thirty minutes after acidic foods before brushing so enamel can re-harden.

Your Child's First Dental Visit

When to book, what to expect and how to help your child feel relaxed.

We recommend a first visit around a child's first birthday or when the first teeth appear. Early visits are short and mostly about getting comfortable.

Talk about the visit in positive, simple terms and avoid words like hurt or needle. Let your child sit in your lap if they prefer. We will count teeth, show them the chair and take our time.

Bring a favourite toy and book a time of day when your child is usually rested.

Five Teeth Whitening Myths, Cleared Up

Does whitening damage enamel? Which products work? Here is what dentists know.

Professional whitening, used as directed, does not damage enamel. Temporary sensitivity is the most common side effect and usually fades within days.

Charcoal toothpastes and homemade pastes are often abrasive and do not change tooth colour in a meaningful way. Whitening gels work on natural enamel only; they do not change crowns or fillings.

Have a check-up before whitening so any cavities or gum problems can be treated first.

How Often Should You Visit the Dentist?

Most adults benefit from a check-up every six to twelve months. Here is how to decide.

The right interval depends on your risk of decay and gum disease. Many adults do well with a check-up every six to twelve months, while some need visits more often.

People with gum disease, a history of cavities, diabetes or dry mouth are usually advised to come more frequently. Your dentist will recommend a schedule based on your last examination.

Book sooner if you notice pain, bleeding gums, a loose tooth or a lump in your mouth.
